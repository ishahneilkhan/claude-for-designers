# The brief bank: four briefs that are not EduBridge

**Reference, not homework.** Four short briefs to practise on when a class says "now do it on something of your own" and you do not have a live client that week.

EduBridge is the taught case and it is deliberately rich: two versions, a PM thread, a background file. **These four are the opposite.** They are thin, vague and underspecified, because that is what most briefs actually look like when they land on a marketplace at 2am.

They are composites, written to match the shape of real listings. No real client, no real posting.

## Why these four

Between them they break every assumption EduBridge might leave you with:

| Brief | What it breaks |
|---|---|
| 1. Dashboard | Not mobile. Desktop, dense, expert users, no consumer polish |
| 2. Storefront | Not a product. A conversion job with someone else's platform constraints |
| 3. Landing page | Not a flow. One screen, one decision, and the copy is the design |
| 4. Booking app | Looks like EduBridge, is not. Different market, different money, different trust problem |

If you only ever practise on a mobile consumer marketplace for a Bangladeshi audience, you will get very good at one job and freeze on the next one.

## How to use them

Pick one and run whichever move the class just taught:

- **Class 1:** write `context.md` for it. Notice how much you have to invent, and mark those lines as guesses.
- **Class 3:** run `/design-brief` on it. Two of these four contradict themselves. Find out which.
- **Class 3:** write the `engagement.md`. Brief 2 has a scope trap in it that will cost you a week if you miss it.
- **Class 7:** write the proposal, including the observation paragraph. Hardest on brief 4.

Copy `_new-client/` for whichever one you pick, exactly as you would for a real client.

## The honest bit

Every one of these is missing something important, and none of them tells you what. That is not sloppy writing on my part. **The gap is the exercise.** A brief that answered everything would teach you nothing, and you will not receive one in your career.
