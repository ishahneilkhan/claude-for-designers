My context block:
- The parent decides to buy the course; the student is the one who
  actually uses it. Design has to satisfy both, not just one.
- Most sessions happen on a shared family phone in the evening, after
  homework, with siblings or TV competing for attention.
- Floor is a mid-range Android phone on 4G that drops to 3G indoors —
  do not assume stable connectivity mid-session.
- Parents are 35-50 and distrust anything that looks like a payment
  trap; they screenshot confirmation pages before trusting a purchase.
- Prior experience is Facebook and WhatsApp, not other EdTech apps —
  navigation patterns should feel familiar from those, not from Duolingo.
- Payment is mostly bKash/Nagad, rarely card; a flow assuming card-first
  will lose them at checkout.
- Trust breaks the moment a fee changes late in the flow — hidden costs
  are the single biggest cause of drop-off.
